WHAT TO TEST — 1.0.2 (4)

Try the new pedal controls in Settings > Live Mode.

Build 4 removes a misleading connection check. Saved keys indicate assignments, not a live Bluetooth connection.

1. Connect your pedal. Keyboard/page-turning pedals pair in iPhone Settings > Bluetooth. Bluetooth MIDI pedals connect through the in-app picker.
2. Assign any of six actions: play/stop, tap tempo, previous/next saved preset, BPM down/up. Tap Learn, then press a pedal key. Tap an assigned key name to change it; tap again while learning to cancel.
3. Try repeated presses, reconnecting your pedal, and reopening the app. Save two presets to test previous/next.
4. Check playback in the background and with the screen locked. Keyboard-mode pedals only control the app while it is on screen. MIDI and media-mode behavior depends on the controller and iOS; please report what works on yours.
5. During a practice session, check what happens after a call or alarm. iOS may interrupt audio. Automatic recovery is conditional on iOS allowing it; this beta does not promise uninterrupted live-performance audio.

Please include your iPhone model, iOS version, pedal brand/model and selected pedal mode in feedback. Note what you pressed, what you expected, and what happened.

BETA REVIEW NOTES

This is a metronome for musicians. No account, login, purchases or sample files are required. Launch the app and press Play. Tempo, meter, subdivisions, percussion sounds, accents, tempo ramps and silent bars can be configured in the app.

New optional functionality: Settings > Live Mode provides six assignable pedal actions. Core metronome functions work without external hardware. Keyboard/page-turning pedals use standard key events while the app is active; compatible MIDI controllers use CoreMIDI. System media commands support playback controls. Previous/next selects existing saved presets, so save at least two rhythms to test this.

There are no external authentication, payment or AI services required for core functionality. Pedal mappings are stored locally. Audio interruptions remain subject to iOS policies; the app only resumes automatically when the system permits it.
